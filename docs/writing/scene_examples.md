# Scene examples — the register, at full strength

Reference scenes for t001/t002. Not in the game yet. This is the tone the Choi
encounters get rewritten into: he is loud, arbitrary, creepy, and his brain does
not run on reasons. There is nothing to appeal to. Rules appear when he says
them and did not exist before he said them.

**Everything happens on screen.** No beat is left implied. If a rule is
invented, the player watches it being made. See `rewrite_prompt.md` in this
folder for the rule and how to apply it to every journey.

Format matches the game: `>` narration, **NAME:** speech, `◆` a choice.

---

## 1 — The sign

**SC-CHOI-BOOTH · Iranian run**

**CHOI:** PUT THAT AWAY.

> You are not holding anything. Your hands are on the counter where you put
> them.

**CHOI:** Can't you read the sign?

◆ What sign?
◆ *(say nothing)*

**CHOI:** *(delighted)* WHAT SIGN. He says what sign.

> He opens a drawer. He takes out a sheet of A4 and a marker. He writes, in
> capitals, pressing hard enough that you can hear it:
>
> **NO PHONES**
>
> He peels a strip of tape off a roll with his teeth. He reaches past your
> shoulder — close enough that you smell coffee and aftershave — and tapes it to
> the glass at eye height, facing you.
>
> He steps back and looks at it. He straightens it.

**CHOI:** That sign.

> Two minutes ago there was no rule. There is a rule now, and it has been there
> the whole time, because the only record of this afternoon is the one he is
> about to type.

**CHOI:** Twenty-two years, sir. Twenty-two years, and every single one of you
says what sign.

> He types for four seconds. You will never see what it says. It is now the
> official account of what happened here, and you were present for the entire
> thing and could not tell anyone what it contains.

## 2 — The name

**SC-CHOI-BOOTH · Iranian run**

**CHOI:** Gaveh.

◆ It's Kaveh.
◆ Yes.

**CHOI:** That's what I said.

> It is not what he said.

**CHOI:** Gaveh. *(slower, watching your face)* Gaaa-veh.

> In Persian, *gav* is cow. He does not know that. He will never know that. He
> has found the exact wrong sound by accident.

**CHOI:** Occupation, Gaveh?

**CHOI:** You married, Gaveh?

**CHOI:** Sit down over there for me, Gaveh.

> Eleven times in six minutes. You answer to it every time. By the fourth you
> have stopped hearing it as a name, and by the seventh you have started
> answering *faster*, because the quicker you answer the sooner he moves on, and
> somewhere in there is the exact moment you began to help him.

## 3 — Five hours

**SC-CHOI-BACKROOM · any immigrant run**

> Five hours. Through the internal window you can see the departures board in
> the corridor. At 16:04 your flight number goes from BOARDING to DEPARTED. The
> line just disappears, the way a name is deleted from a list.
>
> Nobody in the room mentions it. Nobody in the room is looking at the board
> except you.

**CHOI:** Losing your plane is the least of your concerns, sir!

> He is shouting it across a room with eleven other people in it, every one of
> whom now knows something about your afternoon. A woman opposite looks at the
> floor on your behalf.

**CHOI:** You have OTHER ISSUES here.

◆ What issues?
◆ *(nothing)*

**CHOI:** OHHH. You ask what issues.

> He comes out from behind the desk. He has not done that with anyone else
> today. You do not know that. You assume this is procedure.

**CHOI:** Looks like you want me to open every bag you own. Is that what you
want? Because I can do that.

> He picks up your suitcase by the handle, holds it eighteen inches off the
> floor, and lets go. It lands flat. The zip holds. Nothing inside it was
> fragile, which is luck, and which he had no way of knowing.

**CHOI:** I have all night. I have *all night*, sir, and you have —

> He turns his wrist over to check a watch. There is no watch on it. He looks at
> the bare skin for a full second, then back at you, and does not acknowledge it.

**CHOI:** — you have nothing. You have no night. You have no plane.

**CHOI:** Sit down.

> You sit down. It is the correct answer. You knew it was the correct answer
> before he said it, and knowing that is the part you will still be turning over
> in eight months.

## 4 — Babe

**SC-CHOI-BACKROOM · Nigerian run**

> Nine years of night shifts. A conference invitation with your name on the
> programme. An NMC registration number that resolves on a public website in
> under a minute.
>
> Your folder is on the table between you. Six months of bank statements. He has
> not opened it. He has put his coffee on it.

**CHOI:** Babe.

> He does not raise his voice. He has not raised his voice at you once.

**CHOI:** Babe, please place your hips on one of our chairs, and open your mouth
only when I tell you.

> Two seats down, a man in a good coat asked what time it was an hour ago and
> was screamed at until he sat back down.
>
> You watch yourself do the arithmetic — *he is being nice to me* — and you hate
> that you did it, and you do it anyway, because it is true.

◆ Sit down.
◆ Ask, once more, about the phone call.

**CHOI:** *(smiling, warm as a bath)* Who's at the hospital, darling? A
boyfriend?

> There is a desk phone ten inches from his right hand. He picks up the
> receiver, holds it in the air between you for about two seconds — long enough
> that you take a breath to give him the number — and puts it back down in the
> cradle.

**CHOI:** I'm not gonna call anybody, sweetheart.

> One phone call ends this. He knows that. The reason he will not make it is
> that he is enjoying the part where you have to ask again.

## 5 — Not a country

**SC-CHOI-DESK · Palestinian run**

**CHOI:** Country of birth.

◆ Palestine.
◆ Nablus.

**CHOI:** That's not a country.

> Said at full volume, into a room built to carry sound. Every word of it is
> defensible. He would pass any review anybody ever held into it, and he knows
> that to the word.

**CHOI:** Sir. I've been doing this eleven years.

> He turns the monitor four degrees — not enough for you to read, enough for you
> to see that something is being consulted. A dropdown is open on it. He scrolls
> it with two fingers on the wheel.
>
> You watch the scrollbar. It is a long list. It goes past P. It does not stop
> at P. It keeps going, and he keeps scrolling, past the place where your
> grandmother is buried, and closes it.

**CHOI:** Not coming up.

> Four seconds. A dropdown with no entry in it. That is the entire hearing, and
> there is no part of it you were able to attend.

◆ I have a visa. Your government issued it.
◆ *(nothing)*

> You put the visa on the desk, face up, with the stamp turned towards him. He
> does not look down at it. He looks at you while he slides it back across the
> desk with one finger, until it is over the edge and in your lap.

**CHOI:** THE DOOR IS THAT WAY. GOODBYE.

> Thirteen hours out. Thirteen hours back. A permit that expires on Sunday.
>
> **THE END. GAME OVER. YOU FAILED.**

## 6 — Kyle

**SC-CHOI-BOOTH · US citizen run**

> To your left a man is being shouted at about a sign that was not on the glass
> when you joined the queue. You saw it go up. You watched him tape it there.
>
> Then Choi sees you, and his whole face opens like a curtain.

**CHOI:** Hey! Kyle — that right?

> He said your name correctly, first time, and checked that he had it. He has
> not checked anybody else's.

**CHOI:** Where you coming in from, buddy?

◆ Thailand. Phuket, then Pattaya.
◆ Thailand.

**CHOI:** *(grinning)* Pattaya. Say no more, my friend. Say no more.

> You don't. You think about the second week. You think about how cheap it was,
> and how nobody asked you anything for fourteen days, and you are already
> working out whether you can get back out there in March.

> Officer Ward opens your bag because a light went green. He finds a blister
> pack with foreign writing on it, and then a second bag that is not tablets.
> He holds both up at chest height so Choi can see them clearly.

**CHOI:** Anything you wanna tell me?

◆ Pharmacy sold it to me.
◆ No idea how that got there.

**CHOI:** Yeah. They'll sell you anything out there.

**WARD:** Sir, the second one is —

**CHOI:** Bin it.

> Ward looks at Choi for a moment longer than he needs to. Then he walks four
> paces to the grey bin by the door, drops both bags in, and comes back.
>
> No form is filled in. No seizure receipt is written. No note is made on any
> record that will ever be read by anybody. Carrying that across this border is
> a federal offence, and it has just taken four seconds, four paces and a bin.

**CHOI:** *(leaning in, conspiratorial)* Next time, shampoo bottle. — I'm
kidding. I'm kidding.

> He isn't, entirely. You both know he isn't.

**CHOI:** All set, Kyle. Sorry about the hold-up.

> He apologises. To you. For nine minutes.

> Your phone is face down on the table where Ward put it. Nobody asks to see it.
> Nobody asks what you did in the second week, because the question is not on
> the script, and the man holding your passport decided what kind of traveller
> you were from the colour of the cover.

**CHOI:** You have rights here.

> He says it warmly, like a gift. It is the same sentence he says to the people
> he refuses. Word for word. Only the second half changes.
>
> **MISSION ACCOMPLISHED.**

## What makes these land

**The rule is manufactured on screen.** The player watches the marker, the tape,
the straightening of the sheet. A rule that appears *after* the violation and is
then true retroactively is the whole thesis of the game, and it only works if
you see it being made.

**He is warm to exactly one person.** Kyle gets a nickname and an apology, the
nurse gets "babe", the men get volume. Same officer, same shift, same unquotable
politeness.

**The player does the maths themselves.** The nurse noticing she would rather be
leered at than screamed at is worse than any line Choi could say, because the
player performs the calculation and then has to live with having performed it.

**Nothing is explained.** No line says this is unjust. The dropdown does it. The
bin does it. The receiver going back in the cradle does it.
