The `palestinian_women` rows are the point of the dataset. `WOM-002` is the
clearest: a woman in Gaza needs Israel's permission to leave *and*, since
February 2021, can be stopped by a male relative's application to a court —
and HRW notes she is blocked **as soon as he applies**, before any ruling.
Two vetoes, two authorities, and she holds neither key.

## What the brief asked for, and where it landed

- **"Unusually higher rate of random checks at US airport"** → `AIR-001`.
  Screening is name-based; CAIR's analysis of a leaked 2019 FBI watchlist copy
  found the top 50 names all Muslim, >98% of 1.5m entries Muslim, and **174,202
  of 1,566,062 entries containing "MUHAMMAD"**. `AIR-001-005` states honestly
  that **no official per-ethnicity SSSS rate is published** — the claim rests on
  list composition and consistent reporting, not a government statistic.
  `AIR-002` carries the detentions: Alshrouf held **nine days** at Houston on a
  valid immigrant visa against CBP's own 72-hour policy, the SFO visa
  revocations, Ward Sakeik detained returning from her honeymoon.
- **Torture of civilians including nurses and doctors** → the `ATR` category,
  five issues, 33 rows. Dr Adnan al-Bursh, head of orthopaedics at al-Shifa,
  detained 19 Dec 2023, ribs broken at Sde Teiman, died at Ofer on 19 April
  aged 50, never charged, body withheld. PHRI's 24 testimonies (ages 21–69,
  one woman suspended by her wrists for hours). The UN Commission of Inquiry's
  finding that sexualised torture was **policy**, not excess — with Israel's
  rejection recorded in the same row.
- **"Passport doesn't even exist during travel"** → `STA-005` and `STA-006`.
  Not that the passport is weak — that the nationality frequently **is not a
  selectable option**. ISO 3166 files it under P, O or S depending on the
  implementation; the PA biometric passport says PALESTINIAN while forms say
  Palestine; some authorities use ICAO **XXP / X-codes meaning "undefined
  nationality"**, which booking systems reject. And ~340,000 East Jerusalem
  Palestinians travel on an Israeli laissez-passer whose nationality field
  literally reads **"Undefined"**.
- **Psychological effects of being seen as bad, and the cost to efficiency,
  growth and back pain** → `PSY` (6) and `SOM` (4), marked `tier=secondary` so
  they can be filtered. **`SOM-001` is the one with real numbers**: somatic
  symptom disorder in **32.5%** of Palestinian primary-care attendees, painful
  muscles 61.5%, **back pain 52.3%**, female gender **aOR 2.1** — and the study's
  own authors name the occupation as context. `PSY-002` covers the marriage and
  relationship market, including being used as a political accessory and
  discarded, and the custody trap that follows remarriage.
- **Five layers deep** → layers 1–5, with 28 rows at layer 5.
- **Weird and rare examples** → see below.
- **Non-English sources** → German (`STA-004`: VG Regensburg's Dec 2024 refusal
  to naturalise a stateless Palestinian who had never lived in
  Israel/Palestine), Russian (`STA-002`, `REF-003`: the regional
  non-naturalisation rule, stated more directly than in English sources), and
  Chinese (`STA-001`: the fullest passport-recognition list found).
  **yandex.com returns a CAPTCHA to automated access and could not be used** —
  recorded here rather than quietly skipped.

## The weird and rare ones, as asked

- **`ARB-003` — the letterhead.** A woman's permit to visit her husband in
  life-threatening surgery stalled because the Israeli government "doesn't
  transfer documents with the heading *State of Palestine*". The entire
  conflict, compressed into a stationery objection.
- **`SIE-004` — coriander.** From 2007 to 2010 everything was banned unless
  specifically permitted, and the permitted list was never published. Coriander,
  chocolate, toys and notebooks were classed as luxuries. Gisha, on the 2010
  easing: "pleased to learn that coriander no longer represents a threat to
  Israeli security." The same file holds the **"Red Lines"** document — the
  ministry's calculation of the minimum calories a person in Gaza needs — and
  the 2018 list banning wedding dresses, baby bottles and nappies.
- **`ARB-002` — punished for a relative's overstay.** Seven petitions for
  Palestinian Christians denied permits; the ground in all seven was
  "overstay", meaning *a relative* had once overstayed.
- **`DET-004` — smuggled sperm.** No conjugal visits for security prisoners, so
  samples are smuggled out to the Razan clinic in Nablus. Ammar Ziben, serving
  32 life sentences, has a son born 13 Aug 2012. Counts range from 32 to ~120
  babies. Israel then denied those children documents and barred them from
  visiting their fathers.
- **`TEC-001` — a $354 million licence for a network that could not be built.**
  Wataniya won a 2G/3G licence in 2006 and was never able to operate in Gaza at
  all. Jawwal's 4.8 MHz allocation stayed flat while subscribers went from
  120,000 to 2.5 million.
- **`STA-006` — denied a Jordanian visa for being a Jordanian citizen he was
  not.** His travel document listed Jordanian citizenship; he held no Jordanian
  passport; he was refused the visa needed to transit Amman.
- **`EDU-002` — appeal by post, from a place with no post.** Ireland requires
  study-visa appeals by post with original documents. There is no
  internationally recognised postal service in Gaza.
- **`ARB-001` — a security threat twice, never once in front of a judge.** A
  Gaza physician refused permits to sit medical exams on secret grounds; the
  state withdrew both times the moment a petition was filed, once with costs
  awarded against it.
- **`SET-002` — 500 dunums of 5,000, for one day.** Tarqumiya's olive-harvest
  access. In Bani Na'im accessible land fell from ~150 dunums to 12.

## The best-grounded numbers

- **`MOV-001`** — OCHA counted **925 movement obstacles** in Dec 2025
  restricting 3.4m people: 89 checkpoints staffed 24/7, 218 partial, 232 road
  gates, 114 linear closures, 167 earthmounds, 105 roadblocks. **43% above** the
  20-year average of 647. Checkpoints and gates are now ~60% of obstacles
  against 15% two decades ago — the temporary was rebuilt in concrete.
- **`LND-001`** — Bimkom: **24 of 2,550** Area C building applications approved
  2016–20. **282 submitted since Oct 2023, none approved.**
- **`LND-002`** — 1,288 structures demolished Jan–Sep 2025, **4.7 a day**, vs
  3.4/day in 2024.
- **`SET-001`** — 264 settler attacks in Oct 2025, the **highest month since
  records began in 2006**; 1,680 across 2025.
- **`MED-001`** — Gaza exit-permit approvals fell 92.5% (2012) → 47.2% (May
  2017) → 85% (Feb 2023). **Denial is rare; silence is the instrument**: in May
  2017, 47 refused and **1,158 simply unanswered** by the appointment date,
  including 255 children and 141 people over 60.
- **`WRK-002`** — the Bank of Israel put permit-broker profits at **NIS 122m in
  2018**, when 45% of 94,254 workers got jobs through agents taking up to a
  third of wages.
- **`DET-002`** — the US State Department's own 2013 report: **>99% conviction**
  in Israeli military courts.
- **`ATR-004`** — WHO: **735 attacks on health care** (7 Oct 2023–11 Jun 2025),
  917 killed. By mid-2024, health-worker deaths in Gaza **exceeded the combined
  total across all other global conflicts in 2022**.
- **`ATR-002`** — the UN Special Rapporteur on torture: **94 Palestinians died
  in custody** Oct 2023–Aug 2025; of 10 postmortems reviewed, **5 showed signs
  of physical abuse**.

## Honest limits

- **7 of 96 issues carry no `example` row**: `FAM-003`, `WRK-004`, `PSY-001`,
  `PSY-003`, `PSY-005`, `PSY-006`, `SOM-004`. These are aggregate mechanisms
  rather than incidents — a dated "example" would have to be invented, so they
  keep facts and scenarios instead. The other 89 have at least one real sourced
  example.
- **`WOM-001` rests on a narrower base than it looks.** The
  births-at-checkpoints figures (68 births, 35 miscarriages, 5 maternal deaths)
  mostly trace back to a *single* originating source — the Palestinian Ministry
  of Health — relayed by UN agencies and journals, so the apparent corroboration
  is thinner than it appears. Versions disagree (68 vs 69; 2006 vs 2007;
  miscarriages vs infant deaths), and the data concentrates on 2000–2007. The
  caveat is in the row's `notes` field, not just here.
- **`AIR-001`'s watchlist composition is advocacy analysis of a leaked file**,
  not official data. It is used because no official demographic breakdown
  exists — which is itself recorded as a row.
- **232 of 560 rows are `confidence: low`.** That is honest: money and time
  bands are order-of-magnitude estimates unless a `source_url` is present. 249
  rows are `high`.
- **Israeli government positions are recorded where reported** — the IDF and
  Prison Service denials in `ATR-001`, Israel's rejection of the Commission of
  Inquiry findings in `ATR-003`, CBP's "a visa does not guarantee entry" in
  `AIR-002`. The dataset records findings and denials together.
- **yandex.com could not be used** — it serves a CAPTCHA to automated access.
  German, Russian and Chinese sources were used instead, and are cited in the
  rows that rest on them.
- Nothing reaches beyond layer 5, and `EDU` (3), `NAM` (3) and `ARB` (3) are the
  thinnest categories.
