# The psychological effects of war, 1980–2026

The third dataset in the set. Every form war takes — from a genocidal campaign
down to a sanctions regime that never fires a shot — and what each one does to
a mind, temporarily, permanently, and in the generation after.

## Framing rule

This lists **what war does to a mind**, and what that costs in money, time and
energy. Not casualty counts. A death toll is a fact about a war; it is not a
psychological effect.

```
WRONG:  More than 1,000 people were killed in the first week.
RIGHT:  The sirens go at 02:40. By the fourth night nobody goes to the
        basement any more, because the stairs are worse than the odds, and
        the decision not to go is one he will re-examine for years.
```

**`onset`** — 30 `immediate`, 30 `delayed`, 9 `latent_decades`. The last group
is the one everything else misses: delayed keratopathy from mustard gas appears
**15–20 years** after exposure, and the epigenetic and famine findings are read
out six decades later.

`temporary`. Only one effect in the whole file is temporary.

**`generation` / `transmission`** — 52 issues land on the exposed person
(`g0` / `direct`); 17 land on people who were not there, through `in_utero`
(2), `epigenetic` (1), `parenting` (3), `medical` (1) and `social` (9) routes.

## On the generational question specifically

The user-facing claim is that mental illness becomes inherited. The evidence
splits into three, and the dataset keeps them apart because conflating them is
how the topic usually gets ruined.

1. **Epigenetic — real, replicated, much narrower than the headlines.**
   `GEN-001`. Yehuda et al. found altered FKBP5 methylation in Holocaust
   survivors and, in the opposite direction, in their offspring, with parent
   and child levels correlated. The 2020 AJP replication found the effect
   predominated in offspring of mothers exposed *in childhood*, implicating
   **maternal developmental programming rather than germline inheritance**.
   Sample: 32 survivors, 22 offspring, 8 and 9 controls. `GEN-001-006` and
   `-007` state the limits; `NEU-002-005` states plainly that grandchild-
   generation (F2) epigenetic inheritance in humans remains largely unproven.

2. **In utero — the strongest and oldest evidence, and it comes from a war.**
   `NEU-001` and `NEU-002`. Prenatal stress: pooled OR 1.64 for ASD and 1.72
   for ADHD, with I² of 90% and 85% — a real caveat, not a footnote. The
   Swedish register study of 553,766 children found prenatal maternal PTSD
   associated with offspring ADHD at OR 1.79, **partly explained by heredity**.
   Dutch Hunger Winter: IGF2 hypomethylation still readable six decades later,
   schizophrenia risk mediated by DUSP22 rather than IGF2. The same framework
   is now being applied to maternal malnutrition in Gaza.

3. **Social — the strongest generational finding in the file, and no genes are
   involved.** `GEN-002`. The 2026 Halabja three-generation study compared
   cohorts victimised as adults, victimised as children, and not affected. All
   three — including the unexposed — showed elevated depression, PTSD symptom
   severity and sleep disturbance, and **the cohorts were not genetically
   related**. Which means the mechanism is the household, the community and the
   economics (`FAM-001`, `GEN-002-004`) — and therefore that it is
   interruptible.

Nothing here licenses telling an individual family that a war caused their
child's autism. Every one of those rows records elevated population risk and
says where the evidence stops.

## Sources it leans on hardest

- **Chemical weapons**: Balali-Mood & Darchini-Maragheh two-part review; the
  Sardasht-Iran Cohort Study (367 chemically exposed vs 128 conventionally
  war-exposed controls — a genuine control group for isolating chemical effect
  from war effect); the 2026 Frontiers in Psychiatry Halabja study (PTSD 79%,
  depression/anxiety 65%); the 2026 *J Affect Disord* Halabja three-generation
  study.
- **Children**: the 2026 Gaza displaced-children cohort (57.8% CATS-positive,
  15.6% by DSM-5 algorithm, mean ~7 forced migrations); Save the Children 2022;
  Mousa & Madi 2003; the Better Learning Programme (1,093 children, ~half
  nightmare-free after treatment — the one clearly positive finding in the
  file).
- **Continuous traumatic stress**: Straker and the apartheid-era origin; the
  2021 PLOS ONE CTSR validation (factors: exhaustion/detachment, rage/betrayal,
  fear/helplessness — *not* PTSD's clusters).
- **Epigenetics**: Yehuda 2015/2016 and the 2020 AJP replication; Heijmans 2008
  (PNAS) and the 2014 Nature Communications genome-scale follow-up.
- **Sanctions and finance**: Iranian Pharmacists Association (~800 products
  short, 90 essential); peer-reviewed Iranian cancer-care studies; the 2024
  NIAC comment to FinCEN (up to 15,000 Bank of America accounts restricted);
  Armenian Weekly, Mehr/ILNA and Gulf News on Armenia, Georgia and the UAE.
- **Mobility**: Henley and other 2026 passport indices; FY2024 US B-1/B-2
  refusal rate of 55.54%; Proclamation 10998.

## Known limits

- Only 10 `example` rows against 293 `fact`. Named, dated incidents are the
  obvious next pass — especially for the sanctions and de-banking categories,
  where most reporting is aggregate.
- `war_form: kinetic` carries 25 of 69 issues. `siege` (3) and `information`
  (4) are the thinnest and both deserve more.
- Nothing reaches layer 5.
- Confidence is `low` on 230 of 326 rows. That is honest: most of these
  describe mechanisms rather than measured quantities, and the money and time
  ranges are estimates unless a `source_url` is present.
- Geographic skew to Iran, Iraqi Kurdistan and Palestine, because those are the
  best-documented long-run cohorts for chemical exposure, siege and continuous
  threat respectively. Ukraine, Syria, Bosnia, Rwanda and Yemen are
  under-represented.
- `GNC-002-004` deliberately notes that male victims of wartime sexual violence
  are documented almost nowhere. That gap is in the literature, not just here.
