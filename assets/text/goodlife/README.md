## The `triggers` column is the whole design

This is the column that wires this dataset to the other five. Every good thing
carries a list of **hurdle classes it exposes you to** — because in this game
choosing something good is what makes something bad possible.

```python
REQUIRE_TRIGGERS = {
    "none": [],                    "others": [],
    "money_small": ["money"],      "money_large": ["money", "banking"],
    "travel_local":  ["transport", "security"],
    "travel_domestic": ["transport", "security", "police"],
    "travel_intl": ["visa", "border", "airport", "documents"],
    "documents": ["documents", "registry", "discretion"],
    "health": ["health", "care"],  "time": ["work"],
    "place": ["land", "weather"],  "power": ["power", "platform"],
    "institution": ["credential", "discretion", "work"],
}
```

**228 events trigger nothing at all.** These are the pure goods — the ones
nobody can take from you and no border can price. They are the floor of the
game: whatever difficulty the player picked, these remain reachable. Most of
them are in `SEN` (small sensory goods), `HUM`, `RST` and `NAT`.

At the other end, `travel_intl` attaches `visa`, `border`, `airport` and
`documents` to a single choice — which is why `TRV` is the largest category at
192 events and also the one that behaves completely differently depending on the
nationality the player chose at the start. **A Palestinian and a US citizen
picking the identical good thing get different games.** That is the mechanic.

## The five layers

Every event has all five, and they are consistent in meaning across all 889:

1. **What it is** — the plain description of the good thing.
2. **What it does** — the mechanism, usually from the research below.
3. **Anticipation** — Kumar & Gilovich: waiting for an experience produces more
   happiness than waiting for an object, so the looking-forward is itself a good
   and is recorded separately.
4. **The people** — who it is shared with. The Harvard Study of Adult
   Development's finding that relationship quality predicts late-life health
   better than cholesterol is the spine of this layer; it is also why layer 4 has
   930 rows rather than 889.
5. **The memory** — savouring and uplift research: the good thing keeps paying
   out afterwards, and being able to return to it is part of its value.

Shared machinery for layers 3–5 lives in `goodlife_common.py`
(`anticipation_row()`, `people_row()`, `memory_row()`, `expand()`, `TIER_TRIGGERS`,
`TIER_COST`). **It exists specifically to avoid a circular import**: module 1
imports modules 2–6 at the top, so those modules cannot import back from it.
If you add a module 7, put shared helpers in `goodlife_common.py`, not in
`goodlife_data.py`.

## Research the layers are built on

Csikszentmihalyi on flow (the `ART`, `SPT`, `WRK` and `LRN` categories lean on
it); Kumar & Gilovich on anticipation; the Harvard Study of Adult Development on
relationships; savouring and daily-uplift research for layer 5. These give the
`fact` rows their mechanism instead of leaving 4,419 rows as assertion.

## Honest limits

- **0 sourced `example` rows.** Unlike the country datasets, nothing here is a
  reported real-world event. 4,419 rows are `fact` (mechanism, mostly from the
  research above) and 67 are constructed `scenario`. You waived verification for
  this dataset explicitly — but it means this file should never be cited as
  evidence of anything.
- **3,346 of 4,486 rows are `confidence: low`**, the same default as the other
  datasets. Money, time and joy bands are estimates.
- **All 889 are `tier: primary`** — no primary/secondary split.
- **Cultural skew.** The categories and examples lean Anglophone and urban.
  `FTH` (9), `CMT` (10) and `REC` (8) are thin and are the places where that
  shows most; `TRV` at 192 is arguably overweight for a dataset meant to include
  people who cannot travel — though that imbalance is partly the point, since
  `TRV` is where nationality bites hardest.
- **`BIR` (6) and `infancy` (7) are thin**, which matters because the game
  starts at birth. That is the first place to extend.
- **889 is not "thousands"** as the brief asked. You said "889 is fine" when I
  checked. The builder scales — `expand()` in `goodlife_common.py` is how new
  events get their five layers cheaply — so raising it is a matter of adding
  rows to modules 2–6, not restructuring anything.
