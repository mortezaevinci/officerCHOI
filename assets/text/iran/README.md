Confidence: 189 high, 226 medium, 144 low (every `scenario` is `low` by

**18 countries** covered: US, Iran, Canada, Germany, Turkey, UAE, Georgia, UK,
Netherlands, Japan, Australia, Armenia, Italy, Malaysia, PNG, Austria, France,
Sweden.

Three categories exist mainly for the game, because they are where the
day-to-day misery actually lives:

- **`LOG`** (7) — logistics. The appointment that does not exist, the passport
  you must post away for 14 weeks, the courier, the arrival catch-22.
- **`NAM`** (3) — names. Every way a non-English name breaks a form, a credit
  file, a boarding pass or a medical record.
- **`ARB`** (3) — discretion and clerical error. Losing something you were
  genuinely entitled to because one person at a counter got it wrong.

## Best material for the game

**The two hardest documents** — both show selection by demographic category, in
writing:

- `SEC-001` — NSIRA found that before Aug 2018, Canada's mandatory screening
  indicator for Iranian applications was based on **nationality combined only
  with age and sex**. IRCC didn't record which indicator fired, so nobody can
  prove why they were screened.
- `BOR-001` — CBP guidance required secondary inspection for people **born
  after 1961 and before 2001** who were "Iranian and Lebanese nationals".

**The structural trap** (`MIL-001`, `MIL-003`): completed Iranian military
service triggers security referral abroad; *not* serving blocks the Iranian
passport; and since Canada listed the IRGC in June 2024, conscripts assigned to
Sepah units face inadmissibility for "membership". In *Vadiati* the court held
conscript service **can** establish membership. There is no compliant path.

**The best single scene** (`BOR-004`): Shahab Dehghani, Boston Logan, 19 Jan
2020. A visa granted after a year of vetting, revoked by an officer at the
jetway. A federal judge issued an emergency stay. CBP put him on the plane
anyway. The justification — alleged family IRGC ties — reached him through the
press.

**The cruellest finding** (`EMP-001`, layer 5): in DOHA clearance decisions,
evidence that an applicant's family had been *targeted* by the Iranian regime
weighed **against** them, because being targeted proves the regime's interest.
Victimhood as a security liability.

**The strangest** (`DIS-003`): June 2012, an Apple Store in Georgia refused to
sell an iPad to Sahar Sabet, 19, a US citizen, for speaking Farsi to her uncle
— "I just can't sell this to you. Our countries have such bad relations." She
returned with a TV crew and was refused again. The employee was himself
Iranian. The State Department later confirmed no law required any of it.

**Most current** (`EDU-004`): OFAC suspended General License G on 24 Aug 2026.
TOEFL and GRE paused inside Iran immediately; IELTS had already gone on 31 Jan
2026; Duolingo cut off anyone *holding Iranian identification documents* from
1 Sep. Meanwhile the US suspended nearly all Iranian visas from 1 Jan 2026 — so
students now travel abroad and pay to sit a test for a country that will not
admit them.

**A rare quantified male penalty** (`LEG-002`): Germany's protection rate for
substantively examined Iranian claims fell 45% → 37% → 27% across 2023–25, and
within that, men were granted at **25%** against women at **30%**.

## Beyond the US/Canada axis

- **Georgia** (`BOR-005`) — visa-free, and refused at the door. In 2018 Iranians
  were both the largest group holding Georgian residency (7,300, 23%) *and* the
  largest share of the 16,023 refused entry (20%). Students taken off a
  Tbilisi-bound plane after boarding; one described being pushed out "like some
  kind of terrorists". The stated reason is "other cases envisaged by Georgian
  legislation."
- **Netherlands** (`EDU-007`) — from 2008 Iranians were barred from nine degree
  programmes and from URENCO, Petten, Borssele and Delft's test reactor —
  **including Dutch citizens of Iranian descent holding no Iranian passport**.
  They sued and **won**: The Hague court found "no objective and reasonable
  justification". Then the IND quietly halted Iranian visa processing anyway.
- **Australia** (`LEG-003`) — Iran refuses involuntary returnees, so refused
  asylum seekers are detained indefinitely. In *ASF17* the High Court held this
  lawful because he had "the capacity to change his mind" — he had disclosed in
  court that he is bisexual and feared execution. Unanimous. Up to 200 others.
  In January 2026 the UN Committee against Torture found Australia had exposed
  an Iranian detainee to torture.
- **UAE** (`STA-004`) — 2026: residency revoked *while people were abroad*,
  including ten-year golden visas. Non-Iranian family members allowed home;
  Iranian nationals not. Property, bank accounts and children's school places
  all hang off the Emirates ID.
- **Turkey** (`STA-003`) — residence permits refused by postcode, with Iranians
  named among the reasons for the policy.
- **Japan** (`VIS-008`) — the historical rhyme: a 1974 visa waiver, 50,000
  demobilised young Iranian men arriving after the Iran–Iraq war, a community
  improvised in Yoyogi Park, and the waiver cancelled in 1992. 40,001 in 1992 →
  4,300 by 2001.

## Found only in Persian

`VIS-007` — multiple-entry US student visas opened to Iranians in 2010 **but
only for social sciences**. Technical students get single entry. And applicants
are never told which category they fall in: to find out, you must risk the visa
you hold, leave the country, and reapply. This did not appear in any
English-language search.

## The three newest veins

**Logistics** (`LOG-001`): consular appointment slots are hoarded by bots and
resold at EUR 300–500, up to EUR 1,000 urgent and "sometimes as much as 3,000".
VFS's countermeasures include **VPN blocking** — which locks out Iranians, who
need a VPN to reach the site at all. `LOG-002`/`LOG-003`: Iran has had no US
embassy since 1980; the Interests Section inside the Pakistani embassy is a
pass-through that decides nothing, takes 6–14 weeks, and requires you to
**surrender your only passport** for the duration.

**Names** (`NAM-001`–`003`): Mohammad/Mohammed/Muhammad are all correct, and
every system treats the mismatch as two people. The failure list includes the
airline that will not board you, the credit file that splits in two (no
history) or merges with a stranger (their debts), the medical record with
someone else's allergies, the background check that returns "no record found"
and is read as unverifiable rather than clean, and being asked to prove your
own child is yours. Correcting a clerk's typo triggers the change-of-name fraud
checks the typo never did.

**Discretion** (`ARB-001`–`003`): Ontario abolished the 3-month OHIP wait in
2020, so a denial on those grounds is a clerk error — and you are uninsured
anyway. Ontario publishes no rules for maintained status and tells staff to
improvise. RAMQ will not reimburse care from before your start date, so even a
corrected error leaves the bills with you. And the meta-problem: once corrected,
your file contains an inconsistency, which later officers read as
unreliability.

54 constructed cases, written on a small recurring cast so they read as one

> **Hassan**, 58, his father. **Nasrin**, 31, his sister, PR in Toronto.
> **Reza**, 34, Sharif graduate, software, Berlin. **Maryam**, 29, dentist.

Each sits on a documented mechanism from its own issue. Examples: the funeral
Omid watches on a video call at 3am because his F-1 is single-entry
(`VIS-007`); the boarding pass reading HOSSEINI against a passport reading
HOSEINI (`NAM-001`); appendicitis billed at CAD 11,400 because a clerk applied

character referee is a lab manager who met him in September (`ARB-004`); the
registrar in Tehran who simply never replies, so the file stays open and the
Express Entry profile expires with nobody ever having decided anything
(`EDU-001`).

## The secondary tier (`PSY`, 9 issues)

Grounded in real research where it exists, and calibrated where the popular
version outruns the evidence:

- **`PSY-006` the bandwidth tax** — the honest answer to "why don't I have ideas
  any more." Mani et al. (*Science*, 2013) measured scarcity costing roughly **a
  full standard deviation** of effective intelligence and self-control. It causes
  *tunnelling*: the dentist appointment isn't skipped, it's invisible. **And it
  is situational — capacity returns when the pressure lifts.** There's no
  evidence for a direct line from sexual dissatisfaction to fewer business
  ideas; there is good evidence that unresolved preoccupation of *any* kind eats
  the same bandwidth that side projects and risk-taking run on.
- **`PSY-007` the body keeps the score** — allostatic load; a study of 1,714
  migrants in Spain found discrimination → distress → cardiovascular risk, with
  distress mediating. On back pain: plausible but **thinly evidenced** — pain and
  fatigue track *hypo*cortisolism, a different pathway, and standard indices may
  miss it. Also included: the immigrant paradox cuts the other way, and social
  support measurably buffers the physiology.
- **`PSY-009` depicted as something you are not** — Persian is not Arab; Farsi is
  Indo-European, Arabic is Semitic. *Not Without My Daughter* (1991): the NYT
  called it "the stereotype of the demonic Iranian"; Ebert said it made "moral
  and racial assertions that are deeply troubling." A UC Irvine analysis reads
  the husband's violence as framed as **"the pinnacle of Iranian masculinity."**
  Reza Aslan calls the film an "albatross." It was shot in Israel with Russian
  extras. It flopped — and for years was one of the only American films about
  Iran, which is exactly why it mattered.
- **`PSY-004` the intimate market** — with a correction worth keeping: in the
  OkCupid and Facebook datasets, Middle Eastern men are **not** the
  lowest-response group (Black and Asian men are), and the ME sampling is weak.
  The disadvantage is real but smaller than the folk version — and the folk
  version is itself disabling. Stronger finding: between 2009 and 2014 the share
  of users saying they'd date someone with a vocal racial bias fell 27% → 10%
  while behaviour barely moved.
- Also: `PSY-002` the gratitude imperative (scholars argue **no such duty
  exists**), `PSY-003` being counted as a cost, `PSY-001` being read as a threat,
  `PSY-005` asymmetric-status relationships, `PSY-008` what it costs the career.

## Chasing the notes

The `notes` column carried 26 flagged research targets. Nine are now resolved —
search `notes` for **`ANSWERS`** to find them. Two produced entirely new issues:

- **`MIL-004`** — from the IRB's Responses to Information Requests. The military
  service completion/exemption card is a **de facto internal ID**. Without one a
  man cannot get a passport, cannot get a **driver's licence**, is ineligible for
  government jobs and is "not hired for high-paying jobs", and faces "a ban on
  leaving the country without special permission." The exemption card also
  **states the reason** for the exemption — so a medical or psychiatric ground is
  printed on a document used as routine ID. Emigration is blocked at the far end
  of a chain that already blocked everything else.
- **`EMP-004`** — DOJ has repeatedly **fined** employers for the export-control
  exclusions in `EMP-001`. ITAR §120.62 defines "US person" to include permanent
  residents *and* "protected individuals" — asylees and refugees. Clifford Chance
  paid **USD 132,000** for excluding non-citizens *and dual US citizens* (which is
  every Iranian-American, since Iran won't permit renunciation); Honda Aircraft
  **USD 44,626** plus two years' monitoring across 25+ postings; also American
  CyberSystems and General Motors. The postings DOJ names as unlawful are exactly
  the ones people meet: "Only U.S. Citizens", "Only U.S. Citizens or Green Card
  Holders".

Other resolutions worth knowing:

- **`EMP-003`** — the meta-analytic penalty is **29% fewer positive responses**
  for minority-signalled candidates, up to **41% for Arab** applicants. But
  **no study isolates Persian or Iranian names** — they're folded into
  "Middle Eastern"/"Arab/Muslim". That's `PSY-009`'s conflation, baked into the
  research itself. Also: name *pronounceability* is a separate penalty, and
  callback gaps **understate** the real gap (offer-stage disparities are larger).
- **`STA-002`** — exact advisory text. Canada: "you should leave Iran now", and
  **holding a Canadian passport can be reason enough to detain you**. US: dual
  nationals **must exit Iran on Iranian passports** — which chains straight back
  to `MIL-004`, because that passport needs the service card. Australia: the
  government **may not be notified** of a detention at all.
- **`EDU-005`** — the ATAS statistics don't exist publicly *by design*: FCDO
  withholds nationality breakdowns under FOI s.24 and s.27. Also new: **two
  refusals can lead to withdrawal of the study offer**.
- **`PSY-005`** — VAWA self-petition is real and confidential (safe address, no
  police report, abuser never notified) and takes **41.5–46.5 months** for the
  I-360 alone. "Threats to have the victim deported" is itself listed as
  qualifying abuse.
