# Hurdles imposed on anyone, 2000–2026

The **baseline dataset** for `assets/text/iran`. That one lists what is imposed
on an Iranian male immigrant. This one lists what is imposed on *everybody*,
before nationality or status is taken into account.

Generated by `C:\temp\_script\general_hurdles_build.py` from
`general_hurdles_data{,_2,_3}.py` — **edit those and re-run, do not hand-edit
the CSVs.**

```

details/<id>.csv     169 rows                        (23 columns)
source/issues.json   same data as JSON
```

Row types: 130 `fact` · 39 `scenario` (constructed illustration).
Layers: 58 at 1 · 55 at 2 · 40 at 3 · 16 at 4.
Tier: 37 `primary`, 2 `secondary`.

## The admission test: is it IMPOSED?

Immigration hurdles are not chosen. Nobody picks their nationality, and the
queue, the second interview and the extra form arrive regardless of what the
person does. This dataset holds the equivalent for someone who was never an
immigrant: **things that arrive whether you agree to them or not.**

A hurdle gets in only if it is imposed. Every issue carries an `imposed_by`
column saying by what:

| `imposed_by` | Meaning | Count |
|---|---|---|
| `system` | The rules, institutions and markets you were born into and must comply with | 22 |
| `others` | Another person's error, negligence, crime or decision, landing on you | 8 |
| `chance` | Accident, illness, weather, timing — nobody decided it | 8 |
| `biology` | The body and its schedule — ageing, injury, decline | 1 |

**What is excluded, and why.** Outcomes a person arrived at through their own
decisions are not hurdles. General consumer debt is not in here: nobody imposed
it. A *medical* bill is — the illness was not chosen and neither was the
billing system attached to it, which is why `HLT` (you get ill) and `MED` (the
illness generates an invoice) are separate categories with different
`imposed_by` values.

**No population statistics.** This lists what a person has to deal with, not
how outcomes are distributed across a population. "X% of adults cannot cover an
emergency" is a fact about society; it is not a hurdle and it does not belong
here.

```
WRONG:  37% of adults cannot absorb a $400 shock.
RIGHT:  The boiler fails on a Sunday. The only plumber who will come today
        charges a call-out premium, you take a morning off to let him in,
        and the part has to be ordered.
```

Per-incident costs stay: they describe what *you* pay. Prevalence figures do
not.

**Scope rule.** If a hurdle only bites because of nationality, immigration
status or sex, it belongs in `assets/text/iran`, not here.

## How the two datasets relate

Additive, not alternative. Everything here lands on an immigrant too; the Iran
dataset is what lands *on top*. An Iranian applicant proves identity to a bank
**and** to a visa officer, waits in a hospital queue **and** in a security
screening queue. `ADM-001-006` says so explicitly and is the row to quote when
the game needs to make that point.

Same schema and column names as the Iran dataset, so the two can be read side
by side.

| Field | Values |
|---|---|
| `applies_to` | `everyone` (25) · `most_adults` (7) · `situational` (7 — only if you drive, rent, have children) |
| `imposed_by` | `system` · `others` · `chance` · `biology` |
| `tier` | `primary` = the hurdle · `secondary` = its downstream effects |
| `row_type` | `fact` · `scenario` (constructed illustration) |
| `layer` | 1 = the obvious cost · 4 = what it does to the rest of the life |
| `energy_note` | the one-line human cost — the column to mine for dialogue |

## Known limits

- 39 issues against 77 in the Iran dataset, and 169 detail rows against 559.
  Every category could go deeper.
- No `example` rows. The Iran dataset has 195 sourced real incidents; this one
  is `fact` plus constructed `scenario`, because the framing rule rules out the
  aggregate figures that make up most of the published writing on these topics.
  Real named incidents would still be admissible and are the obvious next pass.
- Layer depth stops at 4; nothing reaches layer 5 yet.
- Money ranges are USD and skew to US/Canada/UK cost levels. That is where the
  per-incident price data is, not a claim about where life is hardest.
- `BOD` has a single issue and is the thinnest category; ageing, chronic
  decline and the point at which the body stops cooperating with the work
  deserve more than one row.
