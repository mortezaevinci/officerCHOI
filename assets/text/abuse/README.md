# Forms of abuse, and whether a position of power is required

Built to the same schema as `assets/text/iran`, `palestine` and `nigeria`,
deliberately, so they read side by side. The organising question here is not
*who is hit* but **what the abuser needs in order to do it** — which is why this
dataset carries three columns the hurdle datasets do not.

Generated by `C:\temp\_script\abuse_build.py` from
`abuse_data{,_2…_6}.py` — **edit those and re-run, do not hand-edit the CSVs.**
Run it with **Windows** python (`cd /d C:\temp\_script && python abuse_build.py`);
the output path is a `C:\...` literal and WSL python3 will silently create a
junk directory instead of writing to the game folder.

## The three columns that make this dataset

The brief asked to **specify if it can be done using abuse of position of
power**. That is `power_abuse`, and the answer splits three ways:

| `power_abuse` | Meaning | Count |
|---|---|---|
| `required` | Cannot happen at all without power over the target | 76 |
| `enabled` | Happens without power, but power makes it work and makes it safe | 29 |
| `none` | Needs no position at all — a stranger, a crowd, an account | 3 |

Only three things in 108 need no position whatsoever, and all three are online:
`DIG-002` doxxing, `DIG-003` pile-on, `DIG-004` mass false reporting. That is
the finding, not a gap: **the network is the one place where someone with no
standing can do serious harm.** Everywhere else, harm scales with position.

`power_type` names *which* power is being used — `social` 26, `institutional` 16,
`economic` 14, `authority` 12, `gatekeeping` 11, `information` 11, `discretion` 7,
`custodial` 5, `physical` 3, `clinical` 3. Note that bare `authority` — the
manager who can simply order it — is fourth. Most abuse runs on softer machinery.

`visibility` answers the brief's "do not forget about subtle ones":

| `visibility` | Meaning | Count |
|---|---|---|
| `subtle` | Visible to the target, arguable to everyone else | 66 |
| `deniable` | Constructed so the target cannot prove it happened | 29 |
| `overt` | Nobody disputes what occurred | 13 |

**Thirteen of 108 are overt.** The rest are built to survive being described.
That is the shape of the problem and it drives the game design: the player will
mostly be unable to *prove* the thing that is happening to them.

Two supporting columns: `recourse` (51 `nominal` — a process exists and does not
work · 42 `none` · 15 `partial`) and `triggers_retaliation` (51 yes / 57 no).
The `nominal` majority is the point — the grievance procedure, the complaints
line and the appeal mostly exist to be pointed at (`TOK-002`).

## The examples you named, and where they landed

- **"A man in position of power calling women customers 'babe'"** → **`LNG-001`**.
  Grounded in Glick & Fiske's benevolent sexism — protective paternalism that
  registers subjectively as warmth — and Sue's point that repetition converts a
  microinsult into a microassault. `power_abuse: enabled`, because a stranger can
  say it; power is what makes it unanswerable. The detail rows carry the three
  responses available to her and why all three lose.
- **"US immigration officer denying a TN visa… data scientist is not a valid
  job"** → **`GAT-002`**. Data Scientist is absent from the NAFTA/USMCA Appendix
  1603.D.1 profession list, which has barely changed since 1994; officers apply
  strict categorical matching rather than functional equivalence, so the work can
  be plainly professional and still not be on the list. Port-of-entry decisions
  are final with no appeal.
- **"…on any excuse from transcript"** → **`GAT-003`**, any defect will do: the
  refusal reason is selected after the decision, from a file large enough to
  always contain one.
- **"…not complying with abusive language"** → **`GAT-004`**. Compliance with the
  *tone* as a condition of the outcome. The target must absorb the manner in
  order to obtain the thing, and objecting is itself recorded as the problem.
- **"…or what is the name of end client"** → **`GAT-005`**, the question you are
  not allowed to be unable to answer — where the honest answer ("I don't know,
  I'm placed through a vendor") is structurally indistinguishable from evasion.
- **"Include all obvious abuse but do not forget about subtle ones"** → the whole
  `visibility` axis above, plus the `LNG` category (10 issues), which exists only
  to hold things too small to report: being first-named while he is titled,
  years of mispronunciation, being explained to, interruption and reattribution,
  "calm down", being complimented instead of assessed, the joke you are required
  to enjoy, "you should smile more".

## The villain scenes

You asked that these be carryable by villain characters in the game, specific,
**without terrible carry-out detail**. 87 rows are `row_type: scenario`, marked
in the aspect field as `VILLAIN SCENE`, and written as stageable beats — what is
said, in what order, and what the target is left holding. They are dialogue, not
instructions.

Where I held the line: for `SEX`, `CHI`, `DOM` and custodial `POL`, the rows
record **the power relation, the deniability, the institutional response and the
cost to the target** — never method. `CHI` in particular stays entirely at
institutional-failure level: what organisations did and did not do. There is no
grooming detail anywhere in this dataset, by choice.

The scenes are written to be *quotable*, because that is what makes them playable:

- `SEX-001` — two harmless sentences ten minutes apart, neither of which says
  anything, which together are unambiguous.
- `LEG-003` — your own lawyer works for whoever is paying.
- `REP-002` — the reference call: *"Was she reliable?" "She was never a problem."
  "Would you rehire?" …"That'd depend on the role."* Every word true.
- `FAM-004` — *"You're the one with the flexible job."* She is a district nurse;
  her brother is a project manager working from home three days a week.
- `DOM-003` — nothing prevents her attending the interview. She simply does not
  get there.
- `CAR-001` — the call bell placed just out of reach, nightly, with *"there you
  go"* said as it happens.
- `SUR-002` — *"You've been at your sister's a while x"*. One kiss on the end.
- `PSY-005` — he tops up her glass and says she's been under a lot of pressure,
  and two people tell her afterwards how lucky she is.
- `MED-002` — *"She's a bit of a frequent flyer, this one,"* said in a corridor
  four feet from the cubicle.
- `DIG-002` — three posts, no single rule broken, and she moves house.

## The best-grounded rows

Most of this dataset is structural description rather than event reporting, so
sourced figures are scarcer than in the country datasets. These are the ten
`example` rows:

- **`ELD-001`** — the GAO found the extent of elder abuse by guardians nationally
  is **unknown**, then documented hundreds of allegations across 45 states, and in
  20 closed cases guardians took **$5.4m from 158 incapacitated victims**. In one
  case a guardian with a $31,000 federal tax lien filed a month after appointment
  was allowed to continue; in another, one man served simultaneously as a single
  victim's guardian, conservator, representative payee, therapist, landlord and
  service provider. A prosecutor's observation is the key mechanism: most such
  exploitation happens by **overcharging for services unnecessary or never
  performed** — invoiced, not stolen.
- **`CHI-001`** — inquiry findings that institutions knew: concerns raised and not
  escalated, safeguarding policies in place and not upheld, perpetrators **moved
  on rather than reported**, and some individuals disputing that they held
  "positions of trust" so that safeguarding would not apply to them.
- **`SEX-004` / `POL-003`** — US officers charged with forcible rape **405 times
  2005–2013** (~45/yr) and forcible fondling 636 times; victims typically under
  18; described in the literature as extortion — freedom offered as a
  transaction — and targeted at people whose complaints will not be believed.
  Recorded alongside the finding that **data on sexual assaults by police are
  almost nonexistent**, so these are charges, not incidence.
- **`MED-001`** — a 2024 systematic review's seven themes of medical gaslighting,
  and the measured gap: women with severe stomach pain waited almost **33% longer**
  in emergency departments than men with the same presentation.
- **`ACA-001`** — **24%** of doctoral students reported abuse of power from
  supervisors; 21% reported bullying with **48% naming supervisors**; bullying
  rates higher in academia than other workplaces.
- **`DOM-004`** — **46%** of surveyed survivors reported credit damage caused by a
  partner's actions, and those subjected to coerced debt faced a **sixfold**
  higher likelihood of credit denial.
- **`DIG-001`** — Australia's eSafety reports rose **960%**, 849 (2018/19) → 9,060
  (2022/23), with sexual extortion going 432 → **6,187**.
- **`DOM-001`** — coercive control became an offence in England and Wales under
  s.76 Serious Crime Act 2015 (max 5 years); California folded it into the
  domestic violence definition from January 2021.

## Honest limits

- **Only 10 of 408 rows are sourced `example` rows**, against 44 in the Nigeria
  dataset. This is a real difference in kind: "the reference call that damns by
  omission" is a structure, not an event, and there is no study that counts it.
  87 rows are constructed `scenario`, which you authorised explicitly, and 311
  are `fact` rows describing mechanism. The `row_type` column always says which.
- **313 of 408 rows are `confidence: low`** — the same default as every other
  dataset here. Money and time bands are order-of-magnitude estimates unless a
  `source_url` is present. 81 are `high`.
- **Layer 5 is thin at 14 rows** (against 110/109/108/67 for layers 1–4), and the
  `add()` enrichment pass available in the build script **is not used by any
  module**. A further research layer would go there. This is the clearest place
  the dataset could be deepened.
- **All 108 issues are `tier: primary`.** The primary/secondary split used in the
  Nigeria and Palestine datasets was not applied here, so nothing is marked as
  downstream consequence.
- **Jurisdictional skew toward US and UK law** — the EEOC standards, s.76,
  Appendix 1603.D.1, IICSA. Where a legal test is named, the country is in the
  `country` column; where it is absent the row is `*` and describes mechanism
  rather than law.
- **`CHI` is deliberately shallow at 3 issues** and stays at institutional level.
  This is a scope choice, not an oversight.
- **`FIN-005` (charged more for being poor) has `power_abuse: required` but no
  human decision-maker** — the charge is correct under the terms, applied by a
  system. It is filed as institutional power because the position is held by the
  product, not a person. Same reasoning for `DIG-005`.
- Some issues intentionally duplicate structures in the country datasets from a
  different angle — `SUR-004` against `AIR-001`, `ACA-004` against Nigeria's
  `HAR-001`, `DIG-005` against Nigeria's `DIG-001`. Cross-references are in the
  `notes` column.
