## Two notes on the brief's wording

**"Occupied Mexico."** Mexico is not occupied — it is an independent state. Two
real things sit behind the word, and both are in the data rather than the label:
the 1848 Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo took roughly half the national territory,
which is why Mexicans in the US southwest are in places their families predate;
and large regions live under de facto armed control by criminal groups, which is
territorial power without being occupation (`CAR-002`). The structure you asked
for is preserved; only the word is corrected. Same call as the Nigeria dataset.

**Why a men-first dataset is not symmetrical with the women-first ones.** Men
are the overwhelming majority of Mexico's homicide victims, its disappeared, its
prisoners, its deportees and its workplace deaths — and simultaneously the group
least likely to be read as a victim, least likely to seek help, and most likely
to be read as the threat. Both halves are here. The `MEN` category holds the
second half, because a men-first dataset listing only what is done *to* men
would be missing the part where the expectation of being a man prevents him
doing anything about it.

`VIO-001-005` deliberately records that male and female homicide in Mexico have
**different mechanisms** — nearly one in five female homicides occur in the home
against one in thirteen for men; male killing tracks organised crime, female
killing tracks intimate partners. That row exists so this file cannot be read as
diminishing femicide. `DES-002-003` records the inversion that structures the
whole dataset: **the disappeared are overwhelmingly men; the people searching
for them are overwhelmingly women.**

The cleanest intersection is **`MIL-001`, the ball draw** — the only hurdle in
any of these six datasets where the **statute itself names sex**. Servicio
Militar Nacional registration is obligatory for Mexican men on turning 18 and
voluntary for women; a Sunday in November, a drum, and a coloured ball. White
means Army or Air Force, blue means Navy, black means exempt — except black does
*not* erase military status, it makes him "available" to his zone command.
White or blue is thirteen consecutive Saturdays, 07:00–13:00. Not turning up to
the draw historically gets you a white ball automatically, so absence is not
avoidance.

## What the brief asked for, and where it landed

- **"Unusually higher rate of random checks at US airport"** → `AIR-001`. CBP
  referred **12,660,784 of ~420 million travellers — about 3%** — to secondary
  in FY2024, and officers may refer *with or without any suspicion*; the listed
  grounds explicitly include "random screening" and "officer discretion".
  `AIR-001-003` states plainly that **no per-nationality secondary-inspection
  rate is published by any government**, and that the 3% pools hundreds of
  millions of routine land crossings, so it says almost nothing about any
  specific profile. Same honest treatment as the Nigeria and Palestine datasets.
  Device searches are rarer than the fear of them: 47,047 in FY2024, ~0.3% of
  secondary referrals, under 0.01% of arrivals.
- **"Countries who want to destroy Mexican men and just dump them anyway"** →
  `DEP-001`, and it is documented rather than figurative. **MSF documented 1,267
  people deported into Reynosa after dark in six weeks, 588 of them after
  8pm**, and used the word *dumping*. A University of Arizona survey found **one
  in five** repatriated between 10pm and 5am when services are closed, and
  **39% said US authorities did not return their belongings** — no ID, no
  wallet, no phone (`DOC-003`). Reynosa became dangerous enough that a shelter
  locked deportees *in* rather than out.
- **"Men who don't date Mexican men"** → `REP-002`, and this is one of the few
  places in any of these datasets with **measured** data rather than assertion.
  OkCupid reply rates for male senders: **Hispanic/Latino 25.2 against White
  31.1**. The asymmetry is the finding — Latina women sat at **46.8**, near White
  women's 47.4. The same nationality helps her and costs him. Kevin Lewis's
  analysis of 126,000+ users proposes the mechanism: **preemptive
  discrimination** — people reply across race lines more readily than they
  initiate across them, so he stops messaging before anyone refuses him.
- **"Efficiency, growth, back pain"** → `OCC-002`, `PSY-005`, `SOM-001`. The
  strongest row is **`OCC-002-002`: job precariousness associated with back pain
  at OR 2.09 (95% CI 1.01–4.23)** — insecure work roughly doubles back pain
  independently of the lifting. And **`SOM-001-001` is the most on-brief finding
  in the file**: in HCHS/SOL (n=3,848), chronic and perceived stress were
  significantly associated with allostatic load **for males but not females**.
- **Five more layers** → the `add()` enrichment pass in module 8, which took
  layer 4 from 44 rows to 76 and gave all 78 issues an example or scenario.
- **Weird and rare ones** → see below.
- **Non-English sources** → German, Chinese and Russian material was sought.
  See "What could not be found", which records the failures rather than quietly
  substituting English sources.

## The weird and rare ones, as asked

- **`VET-001` — men deported by the army they served in.** The Deported Veterans
  Support House in Tijuana, "the Bunker", founded 2013 by Hector Barajas, a
  deported 82nd Airborne paratrooper, in a two-storey building attached to a tyre
  shop, with portraits of deported servicemen up the staircase. By 2017 he had
  identified **350 deported US veterans born in more than 30 countries**. No
  government publishes the number. Barajas became a citizen only after a **pardon
  from Governor Jerry Brown in 2018**. The detail that lands hardest:
  `VET-001-007` — deported veterans frequently remain eligible for burial in a US
  national cemetery, so the state that would not let them live there will accept
  them once dead.
- **`DIS-003` — Mexico's own massacre and mass expulsion.** **308 Chinese people
  killed in Torreón over ten hours, 13–15 May 1911**, roughly half the city's
  Chinese community, by troops aligned with Madero. An investigation found it an
  unprovoked act of racial hatred; China demanded 30 million pesos. **López
  Obrador apologised in 2021.** Then the **Sonora and Sinaloa expulsions of
  1931–33**: several thousand Chinese men and their Mexican families expelled,
  some **pushed through gaps in the international boundary fence** into the US,
  whose agents deported them onward to China. Mexican women were stripped of
  citizenship for marrying Chinese men.
- **`CAR-001` — the job advertisement that is a trap.** Rancho Izaguirre,
  Teuchitlán, found **5 March 2025 by Guerreros Buscadores, a civilian search
  collective — not the state**. Fake security-guard ads at **MX$4,000–12,000 a
  week**, well above market, across at least 60 social media pages; applicants
  told to gather at bus terminals. Many did not know who was hiring until they
  arrived. In 2024, **30 young people disappeared after fake job interviews in
  San Pedro Tlaquepaque**. Ten men were sentenced to **141 years each** in July
  2025; the mayor was detained.
- **`MIG-002` — the suitcases that were not collected.** The 2011 San Fernando
  massacre — **193 people, 47 clandestine mass graves** — came to light because
  luggage went unclaimed in Reynosa and Matamoros. The 2010 massacre of **72
  migrants** was discovered only because one wounded survivor walked to a
  checkpoint. Federal prosecutors indicated **local police helped intercept the
  buses**.
- **`DES-002` — the method is a steel rod and a smell.** Search collectives push
  rods into the ground and smell them. That is how the graves are found, and it
  is done by relatives because it is not done otherwise.
- **`MIG-003` — Guangdong to Tijuana via Quito.** Border Patrol encountered
  **330 mainland Chinese migrants in FY2021 and 24,050 in 2023**; many flew to
  Ecuador (no visa required), then to Tijuana, paying ~$400 for a drive to a gap
  in the fence, or $7,000–10,000 for Darién Gap routes.
- **`BOR-001` — more people died in the Sonoran than in the Sahara.** 307 of the
  686 US-Mexico border deaths IOM documented in 2022 were desert crossings,
  against at least 212 in the Sahara.
- **`REP-004` — the accent that was a scarlet letter becomes the wage.**
  Tijuana's call centres grew from ~10 to **more than 60** in two decades, most
  employing deportees, drawing work from India on accent-neutral English at
  Mexican wages. One recruiter: *"Tijuana has become like the new Bombay."*

## The best-grounded numbers

- **`VIO-001`** — of 31,062 homicide victims in 2023, **27,221 were men (87.6%)**.
  Across 1990–2022, **538,996 of 605,260 homicides (89.05%) were male**;
  age-adjusted rate 41.55 per 100,000 for men against 5.48 for women.
- **`DES-001`** — **76.83%** of 128,713 registered disappeared were men (IACHR,
  June 2025); Sinaloa 92%, Jalisco 86%. Men 20–34 and women 19–24 are the most
  disappeared groups. Registry totals range from 130,010 to 132,534 missing
  depending on cut and date.
- **`OCC-001`** — Hispanic/Latino workers had the **highest fatal injury rate of
  any group for the eighth consecutive year** at 4.3 per 100,000 FTE (2024),
  against 3.3 overall; **68.5% of the 1,229 deaths were foreign-born**. In 2021,
  foreign-born Hispanic workers were **27% of all construction deaths**, up from
  19.8% in 2011.
- **`POL-001`** — **42%** of incarcerated people reported being beaten to
  incriminate themselves (ENPOL 2021); **79%** were tortured or mistreated
  2006–2016 (WJP/INEGI, n=51,658). No family contact raises torture frequency
  **16%**; no lawyer, **21%**.
- **`JUS-001`** — **42.2%** of prisoners had no sentence at end-2025; of 97,722
  unsentenced, **50.1%** were under automatic pretrial detention. **Estado de
  México and Michoacán apply it to 100%** of their unsentenced population.
- **`ECO-001`** — informality **55.1%** (Q2 2026), essentially unmoved for two
  decades, alongside the **lowest unemployment rate in the OECD at 2.4%**.
  **45.7%** of the employed earn minimum wage or less.
- **`ECO-002`** — Mexico is **second in the OECD for hours worked**, at 42–43
  per week for twenty years.
- **`DIS-001`** — lightest-skin Mexicans average **1.4 more years of schooling
  and 53% higher hourly earnings** than the darkest (Campos-Vázquez &
  Medina-Cortina, *Demography* 2019). The penalty gradient is **steeper for men**
  at the bottom of the origin distribution.
- **`MEN-001`** — male suicide **8.7 per 100,000 against 2.2** for women.
- **`MEN-002`** — traditional machismo correlates with depressive symptoms at
  **r = .243** (8 studies, N=3,413) — and **not** with greater willingness to
  seek help.
- **`REM-001`** — **$61.79bn in 2025, down 4.6%**, the first fall after eleven
  years of growth; **3.4% of GDP**; real purchasing power down **14.4%**.
- **`EXT-001`** — **97% of extortion goes unreported**; Coparmex put the cost to
  business at **~$1.3bn in 2023**; ANPEC estimates **10–20% of income** for
  affected small businesses at 500–1,000 pesos a week.

## What could not be found

Recorded rather than papered over with substitutes:

- **Chinese state media did not cover the Chinese migration route through
  Mexico.** Searching Xinhua, CGTN and Global Times for coverage of Chinese
  migrants at the US-Mexico border surfaced nothing; the reporting is Western
  and Hong Kong outlets. Recorded at `MIG-003-002`.
- **yandex.com returns a CAPTCHA to automated access** and could not be used —
  the same outcome as the Nigeria dataset.
- **No published per-nationality secondary-inspection rate exists** (`AIR-001-003`).
- **BLS publishes no fatality *rate* by race for specific industries** — only
  counts. There is no published fatality rate for Hispanic construction workers,
  and anyone quoting one has invented it (`OCC-001-004`).
- **No single study combines perceived discrimination, cortisol and hypertension
  in Latino men** within HCHS/SOL; the chain at `SOM-001` is assembled from
  adjacent analyses and says so (`SOM-001-004`).
- **The tattoo-as-employment-barrier claim for deportees is unsourced.** Widely
  described anecdotally; targeted searching found no documentation. Marked
  `confidence: low` at `REP-004-005` rather than asserted.
- **Scholarship on why male victims receive less investigative attention is
  thinner than the phenomenon.** Femicide in Mexico is well theorised and well
  counted; the male equivalent is not. Recorded as a gap at `MEN-003-004`.

## Honest limits

- **46 of 367 rows are constructed** (`row_type: scenario`). You authorised this
  explicitly. They are plausible, labelled, and never dressed as sourced — and
  32 of them exist because the build's own coverage check flagged 32 issues
  carrying only `fact` rows on the first pass.
- **211 of 367 rows are `confidence: low`** — money and time bands are
  order-of-magnitude estimates unless a `source_url` is present. 108 are `high`,
  the strongest ratio of the six datasets.
- **The 89% figure at `VIS-001-002`** (214(b) share of Mexican B-1/B-2 refusals)
  comes from an immigration law firm, not State Department data, and is labelled
  `confidence: medium` with that caveat in its `notes`.
- **Disappearance totals are politically contested.** The registry is "depurada"
  (cleaned); estimates suggest at least 1.5 unreported cases per reported one;
  one source reports the 2025 sex split shifting to 62.84% men against the
  historic 76–77%. `DES-001-004` records the dispute rather than picking a side.
- **`DIS-003` is historical (1911–1934)** and sits outside the dataset's
  2000–2026 era. It is included deliberately, flagged in `era_note`, because the
  brief asked for rare material and because it is context for `DIS-001`.
- **`MIL-001` is the only issue in the `MIL` category** and `VET-001` the only
  one in `VET`. Both are single-issue categories by design, not by omission.
- **Geographic skew** toward the border states, Jalisco, Guerrero and Michoacán,
  and toward US destinations, because that is where the data is collected. The
  Mexican south and the Guatemalan border are under-covered.
- **Only 3 issues sit on the bare `men` axis** (`MIG-001`, `MIG-002`,
  `MEN-003`) — hurdles that hit men regardless of nationality. The axis is real
  but thin; most of this file needs both terms.
